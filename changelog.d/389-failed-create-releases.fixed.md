- A failed `AgentFramework.create()` now releases what it had acquired before its error reaches the caller, so the same process can call `create()` again (#389). Before, a `create()` that failed after opening its store, such as on an invalid `timeZone`, an MCPL `toolPrefix` collision or an out-of-range `toolResultInlineMaxChars`, left the store locked: every later `create()` on that `storePath` got `Store is locked by another process` until the process exited. It also left running what it had started, the liveness watchdog among it, which could then kill a process that carried on after the failure. Now the framework it had begun is stopped as `stop()` stops one (its modules, MCPL servers, event gate, tune-out coordinator and watchdog), and a store it opened from `storePath` is closed. A store passed in as `store` stays open.
  - The caller of a failed `create()` has no framework to give `stop()` a deadline, so `create()` waits at most 30 s for each part of the release, where `stop()`'s default has no deadline. A module whose `stop()` never settles would otherwise turn a failed boot that exits into one that hangs.
  - When the release fails or doesn't finish within that time, `create()` rejects with an `AggregateError` holding its own error first and then the release's. Its message begins `AgentFramework.create() failed:` and says that part of what it started may still be running. The store is closed even then. When the release finishes cleanly, `create()`'s own error is rethrown unchanged.
