- The workspace's `edit` matches and replaces through a planner ported from hands-mcp's, and says more when it can't apply:
  - A miss shows the stretch of the file most like the first line of `oldString` with two or more characters, numbered as `read` numbers lines, and says to match exactly. A file over 2,097,152 UTF-16 code units gets no such region, and the refusal says to find the text with `grep`.
  - A count other than one, without `replaceAll`, names the lines of up to 20 occurrences, each line once, and shows each of those lines, a long one as an excerpt around its match.
  - Occurrences that overlap one another each count, so "aa" occurs twice in "aaa", and can't each be replaced: an edit whose occurrences overlap is refused, with `replaceAll` too. Since it's refused whatever their number, overlapping occurrences are counted only until the refusal has the 20 it shows, and it says "at least".
  - An `oldString` with LF line ends matches a file's CRLF ones, and the new text takes, by position, the line ends of the text it replaces. A BOM, a lone CR and every byte outside the replaced text stay as they were. Before, an LF `oldString` missed in a CRLF file, and a CRLF one could mix the file's line ends.
  - When `oldString` isn't in the file as given, straight and curly quotes are matched as equal, and the answer says so.
  - When it still isn't, an `oldString` that starts with the file's BOM or ends with a line's CR, as a character page shows them, is matched without them: the BOM only at the file's start, and the CR only before a CRLF line end. A BOM or CR at the same end of `newString` is read the same way, so the file keeps its one BOM and its line end, and the answer says so. An edit whose only change, read this way, is the BOM or CR is refused as one with nothing to change. Before, such an `oldString` matched the file's raw text, BOM and CRs included.
  - `oldString` and `newString` that are the same, once CRLF line ends are read as LF, are refused before anything is read. Where they differ only in their line ends, the refusal says that an edit keeps the file's line ends and that `write` replaces the whole file, line ends included. Before, such an edit changed the line ends of the text it replaced.
  - An edit that would take a file past its mount's `maxFileSize` is refused, as `write` refuses content past it, and the refusal says the edit must leave the file within the limit. One that its counts alone take past the limit, such as a `replaceAll` that lengthens millions of occurrences, is refused before its new text is built, and the refusal says the file would be at least the bytes its new text's length shows. Before, such an edit was built whole, and one past V8's longest string was refused with "Invalid string length".
  - A path not spelled plainly in its mount, with an empty, `.` or `..` segment, a trailing `/`, or on Windows a backslash, such as `work/sub//b.txt`, is refused, naming its plain spelling, since the workspace would keep it as an entry apart from the file's own. One that names the mount's root, such as `work/.`, is refused as not a file, and one whose part in the mount starts with `/` is refused as outside its mount, as before. Before, `edit` took such a path as written.
  - A miss or a count that differs is refused with "The edit did nothing:", where it said "String not found in …" or "String found N times in …". What the edit's settling with disk took in follows the problem after a blank line, where it followed on the same line.
- A successful `edit` answers with `replacements` and `changed` beside `path` and `size`: the changed region with two lines on each side, numbered as `read` numbers lines, a line over 2,000 characters from at most 80 characters before the change on it. Its `note` also says, where they apply, that the file's CRLF or mixed line ends were kept, that the text matched with quotes treated as equal or without the BOM or a line end's CR it carried, and on which lines `replaceAll`'s other replacements are.
- A `write`, `edit` or `delete` whose store throws after recording the change, as Chronicle 0.4 does when the snapshot it writes after a record fails, answers that the change is stored and mustn't be sent again, and that the store failed after recording it, with its error. On a mount with `autoMaterialize` it says the push to disk didn't run, and that a materialize of the path takes the change there (with `applyDeletions` for a deletion). Before, it answered with the store's error alone, as a `write` of the bytes its path already holds still does, since reading the path back can't show whether the store recorded it. Where the change can't be read back either, `edit` says whether it's stored isn't known, and to read the file before sending the edit again.
- A call that would change an agent's own generated file, such as a `write`, `edit` or `delete`, is refused with the tools that change what the file shows, `set_tool_visibility` and `set_tool_description`, where the refusal said to edit the presentation configuration.
- `edit`'s note on a conflict says what a sync of the path does, from what disk holds at the call, whatever kind the conflict was recorded as: where disk no longer has the file, a sync takes disk's deletion, which removes the workspace's copy; where disk has one, a sync keeps disk's copy and gives up the workspace's. Either way, it says, a forced materialize writes the workspace's copy to disk.
- `edit`'s description and its parameters' descriptions changed, and the workspace has a new tool, `edit_many`, so the first request after the upgrade that carries the workspace's tools misses the prompt cache from its tool block on.
- `edit`'s refusal and receipt are held to the call's `resultMaxChars`, or with none to 24,000 characters, so that a closest region or a long receipt doesn't push them past what reaches the agent whole: past it, a refusal's problem keeps its first line, and the receipt names a region by its lines rather than showing it. A receipt whose path and notes alone pass the cap keeps them and shows no region.
